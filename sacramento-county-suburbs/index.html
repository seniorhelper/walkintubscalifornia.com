Most of the calls we get from these suburbs sound alike. A parent has lived in the same single-story house for forty years, the hall bath still has its original tub, and climbing over that wall has started to feel like a gamble. The good news is that postwar ranch houses are some of the most forgiving homes in California for a walk-in tub. The details that change a quote are local: which office permits the job, how hard the water is, and what is hiding under a sixty-year-old tub.

Who issues the permit, the county or the city?

In the unincorporated communities, Sacramento County Building Permits and Inspection handles the job. Its permit list names "Shower/tub encl replacement" and water heaters among residential work that needs a permit, so a walk-in tub swap, which touches the drain and supply lines, should be permitted. Applications go through the county's Citizen Access portal online, and inspections are scheduled through the county's automated line at 916-875-5296. If the tub has jets or a heater, the new circuit is on the permit too.

Rancho Cordova and Citrus Heights are incorporated, so their own building departments issue the permit. Citrus Heights publishes an unusually clear chart of which projects need one; our Sacramento region guide walks through it. Whoever issues it, ask the installer to list the permit on the quote and to schedule the final inspection, and keep the signed card with the tub paperwork.

For any work over California's minor-work limit, the person doing it should hold a CSLB license. Our CSLB contractor checklist shows how to look one up in two minutes.

Is the water hard in Rancho Cordova and Arden-Arcade?

It depends on which utility sends the bill, and in these suburbs that can change from one street to the next. Two published reports show the range. Golden State Water's Arden system reports an average hardness of 160 parts per million (about 9.9 grains per gallon), with samples from 110 to 240. The Sacramento County Water Agency's North Service Area, a blend of groundwater from the Mather, Sunrise and Anatolia system with surface water from the Vineyard plant, reports about 52 ppm for surface water and 55 ppm for wells. On the agency's own scale, 0 to 60 is soft and 121 to 180 is hard.

What it means for a walk-in tub: on hard water, wipe the door gasket after each bath, run the jet cleaning cycle the manual calls for, and expect the faucet aerator to need a soak in vinegar now and then. On soft water, scale builds slowly and a softener is rarely worth buying for the tub alone. Sacramento Suburban Water District, California American Water and the cities' own systems serve other pockets of the area, each with its own report.

What should you check in a postwar ranch house?

North Highlands grew up beside McClellan Air Force Base, which closed in 2001, and Rancho Cordova grew around Mather Air Force Base and nearby aerospace plants. Many homes from those decades are single-story, often with one full bath and one half bath. That layout is a gift for aging in place, because nobody has to climb stairs to bathe, but the bathrooms are small and original. Before the call, look at four things:

  • Doorway width. Many hall bath doors from that era are narrow. Measure the opening, because the tub has to pass through it.
  • The floor at the tub. Press on the floor in front of the old tub. A spongy spot means subfloor repair.
  • The water heater. Note the gallons on the label. A deep tub can empty a small tank before it is full.
  • The electrical panel. Look for open breaker slots if you want jets or a heated seat.

What help is there for veterans and Medi-Cal members?

  • Veterans. The Sacramento VA Medical Center is in Mather, on the former base, with prosthetics services by referral. Ask there about the HISA grant before you sign anything: it is a lifetime benefit of $6,800 for service-connected conditions or $2,000 for other conditions, it names roll-in showers as an example, and it excludes spa and hot-tub features.
  • Medi-Cal members. Some Medi-Cal managed care plans offer home accessibility changes through CalAIM Community Supports. Our Medi-Cal home modifications guide explains who qualifies and how to ask the plan.
  • Local aging help. Agency on Aging Area 4 serves Sacramento County; its contacts are on our Sacramento page.
  • Medicare. Original Medicare generally does not pay for walk-in tubs. See paying for a walk-in tub in California.

Questions people ask

How much does a walk-in tub cost in Rancho Cordova or Arden-Arcade?

Aging Safely Baths publishes one installed range, $16,500 to $20,500 and up after the current promotion; your free written quote depends on the bathroom and the model. The model, a new circuit for jets or a heater, and wall or floor repair decide where your quote lands.

Who issues the permit in North Highlands, Foothill Farms or Rio Linda?

Sacramento County does, because those communities are unincorporated. The county lists shower and tub enclosure replacement and water heaters among the jobs that need a permit, and applications run through its Citizen Access portal. The licensed installer normally pulls it.

Does Rancho Cordova have its own building department?

Yes. Rancho Cordova and Citrus Heights are incorporated cities, so permits go through the city, not the county. Our Sacramento guide covers the Citrus Heights permit chart in detail.

Is the water hard in the Sacramento suburbs?

It depends on the provider on your bill. Golden State Water's Arden system reports an average of 160 parts per million, which is hard, while the county water agency's North Service Area reports about 52 to 55, which is soft. Hard water means more wiping of the door seal and jets.

How long does a walk-in tub install take in a ranch house?

Most installs take one day once the tub is on site. Single-story ranch houses with a standard 60-inch tub space are usually the simplest swaps. Moving a drain in a slab, adding a circuit or repairing a soft subfloor can add time, and the quote says so.

Can veterans in Rancho Cordova get help paying for a walk-in tub or shower?

Possibly. The VA HISA grant is a lifetime benefit of $6,800 for service-connected conditions or $2,000 for other conditions, and it names roll-in showers as an example. Prosthetics at the Sacramento VA Medical Center in Mather is the place to ask, before work starts.
