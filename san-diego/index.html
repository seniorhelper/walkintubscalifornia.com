Walk-in tubs in San Diego County: what local families should plan for

From Oceanside to Chula Vista and inland to El Cajon and Ramona, San Diego County homes share one detail that matters for walk-in tubs: hard water. Here is how that, the coastal climate and local programs affect your plans.

San Diego County stretches from the beaches to the mountains to the desert, and so do its bathrooms. A beach cottage in Ocean Beach, a 1960s single-story in Rancho Bernardo, a two-story in Chula Vista's Eastlake and a manufactured home in an Escondido park each call for a slightly different plan. This guide focuses on what is particular to San Diego.

Hard water and walk-in tubs

Much of San Diego's water is imported, and the City of San Diego reports an average hardness of about 16 grains per gallon, roughly 276 parts per million. That is hard water. Over time, minerals can leave scale on faucets and drain parts and inside jet lines, and they can make the area around the door seal harder to keep clean.

  • Ask about jet cleaning. Follow the manufacturer's cleaning routine and schedule. Air jet systems hold less water in their lines than water jets.
  • Wipe the door seal area regularly with a cleaner the manufacturer approves, to prevent mineral buildup that can affect the seal.
  • Consider your water heater. Scale also builds up inside tank water heaters. If the heater is old and the tub will rely on it for a deep fill, have it checked.
  • Ask about a water softener only after confirming it suits your household's health needs. Some people on sodium-restricted diets avoid softened water for drinking; talk to a doctor.

Your water district's annual water quality report gives the figures for your address if you live outside the city.

Coastal homes: ventilation matters

Near the coast, the marine layer keeps air damp for much of late spring and early summer. A walk-in tub adds warm water and longer baths to a small room. A good bathroom exhaust fan, used during and after the bath, helps prevent mildew around the door seal and on walls. If the bathroom has only a window, ask about adding a fan while the walls are open.

Inland homes and the heat

In El Cajon, Santee, Ramona and Escondido, summers are much hotter than at the beach. In a hot house, a long warm soak can leave some people light-headed, especially those on blood pressure medicines. Keep the bathroom cool, sit for a minute after draining, and stand up slowly. Ask the doctor if warm baths are right for the person.

Housing types across the county

Mid-century single-story homes

Clairemont, Allied Gardens, San Carlos and much of East County were built out in the 1950s and 1960s with single-story homes. These often have a hall bath with a standard tub that can be a good candidate for a direct swap.

Retirement-oriented communities

Rancho Bernardo and other areas have many older residents, often in single-story homes and condos. Expect association approval requirements in many of them.

Manufactured homes

San Diego County has many mobilehome parks. Bathrooms tend to be compact, floors may need checking for the weight of a filled tub, and permits in many parks come from the state Department of Housing and Community Development rather than the city.

Newer two-story homes

In Eastlake, Otay Ranch, San Marcos and 4S Ranch, the primary bath is often upstairs. A first-floor bathroom may be the better place for a walk-in tub or shower if stairs are becoming hard.

Local aging resources

Aging & Independence Services (AIS), part of the County of San Diego Health and Human Services Agency, lists its call center as 800-339-4661. AIS offers information and referrals, care coordination and caregiver support.

AIS also runs the county's Dignity at Home fall prevention program, which can provide free home safety products and assistive devices to eligible residents who are 60 and older or have a disability, live in San Diego County and are at risk of falling. It is not a walk-in tub program, but grab bars and bathroom equipment can make a big difference while you plan a larger change.

Veterans in San Diego

San Diego has one of the largest veteran populations in the country. Veterans should contact the VA San Diego Healthcare System's prosthetics service about the HISA benefit before buying. VA lists walk-in and roll-in showers as eligible examples, while its exclusions include spa, hot tub and Jacuzzi-type tubs, so approval for a specific walk-in tub is not guaranteed. Permits

The City of San Diego's Development Services Department handles permits within the city. Chula Vista, Oceanside, Escondido, Carlsbad, El Cajon and the county's other cities run their own building departments, and unincorporated communities go through the County. Requirements depend on the scope of work, so let your installer confirm the details for your address.

Questions people ask

Does San Diego's hard water affect a walk-in tub?

It can. The City of San Diego reports average drinking water hardness of about 16 grains per gallon. Minerals can build up on jets, faucets, drain parts and the door seal area over time. Follow the manufacturer's cleaning schedule, and ask whether the tub's jet system has a purge or cleaning cycle.

Is there free fall-prevention help in San Diego County?

The county's Aging & Independence Services runs a Dignity at Home fall prevention program that can provide home safety products and assistive devices to eligible residents who are 60 or older or have a disability and are at risk of falling. Contact AIS to check current eligibility and availability.

Can a walk-in tub go in a mobile home in San Diego County?

Sometimes. Measure carefully, check the floor structure, and ask the park office which agency issues alteration permits. In many California mobilehome parks that is the state Department of Housing and Community Development.

Who issues permits in San Diego?

Inside the City of San Diego, the Development Services Department handles building permits. Other cities such as Chula Vista, Oceanside, Escondido and Carlsbad have their own departments, and unincorporated areas go through the County. Your installer should confirm which applies.
