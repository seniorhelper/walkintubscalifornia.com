Before you read the answers

Three ideas run through almost every answer below. First, the right choice depends on the person more than the product: how they move, how they like to bathe, and how their needs may change. Second, the house matters: the size of the tub opening, the water heater, the electrical panel and the foundation all shape the job. Third, in California, local rules matter: your city or county sets permit requirements, your association may have its own approval process, and state programs each have their own eligibility rules.

Questions people ask

How long does a bath in a walk-in tub take?

Longer than in a standard tub, because you sit in the tub while it fills and again while it drains. How long depends on the tub size, your water pressure, the drain system and your home's drain line. A fast-drain option and a heated seat make the waiting more comfortable.

Can two people use the same walk-in tub?

Most walk-in tubs are designed for one bather at a time. If a couple both want to use it, check that the seat height, interior length and controls suit both people.

Can I still take a shower in a walk-in tub?

Many models include or can be fitted with a hand-held shower. You shower seated with the door closed. In California, new showerheads, including hand-held ones, are limited to 1.8 gallons per minute.

How much water does a walk-in tub use?

It depends on the model. Walk-in tubs are deep, so a full bath can use more water than a standard tub. Ask for the model's fill capacity and compare it with your water heater size.

How do I clean the jets?

Follow the manufacturer's cleaning instructions, which usually involve running the jets with a cleaning solution on a regular schedule. Air jet systems hold less water in their lines than water jet systems, so they need less effort.

Do I need a permit in California?

It depends on your city or county and on the scope. New electrical circuits, moved plumbing, and water heater replacement typically involve permits. Your installer should tell you what applies and who pulls the permit.

I rent my home. Can I install a walk-in tub?

Only with the property owner's written permission. Medi-Cal home modification programs also require owner consent for renters. Some tenants with disabilities may request reasonable modifications under fair housing law; a local fair housing organization can explain your rights.

I live in a condo or HOA community. What should I do first?

Read your association's rules and ask about the approval process before you sign a contract. Many associations require written approval, contractor insurance certificates and specific work hours.

Will a walk-in tub fit in a mobile home?

Some models are sized for smaller bathrooms, but floor strength and access are important. In many California mobilehome parks, alteration permits are issued by the state Department of Housing and Community Development rather than the city.

What if the person who will use the tub has dementia?

The routine of a walk-in tub has several steps that must happen in order. Some people with mild memory loss manage well with practice and supervision. As memory loss progresses, a roll-in or walk-in shower may be simpler and safer to supervise.

Can I have a walk-in tub shipped to my home without installation?

Ask about delivery only. Aging Safely Baths & More can ship a tub for your own licensed contractor to install. Make sure your contractor reviews the installation instructions and plans the drain and electrical work first.

Will Medicare pay for my walk-in tub?

Original Medicare generally does not pay for walk-in tubs, because they are treated as home modifications rather than durable medical equipment. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer extra home-safety benefits; call your plan.

Is there a grant for veterans?

The VA's HISA benefit can help with medically necessary home changes. VA lists walk-in and roll-in showers as eligible examples and excludes spa, hot tub and Jacuzzi-type tubs, so get approval from your VA medical center before buying a tub.

How do I know the installer is licensed?

Ask for their California contractor license number and look it up on the CSLB website. Confirm it is active, matches the business name, and shows bond and workers' compensation information.

No. This website is an information and quote site. Quotes, products and installation are provided by Aging Safely Baths & More, which serves California through factory-certified installers and offers delivery statewide. We do not have showrooms or offices in California cities.
