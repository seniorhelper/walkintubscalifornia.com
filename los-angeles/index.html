Walk-in tubs in Los Angeles: a guide for LA homes and families

Los Angeles bathrooms range from 1920s tile in a Craftsman bungalow to a 1990s condo in Koreatown. This guide covers what LA homes tend to need, where to find local aging help, and how to plan around permits and HOAs.

Los Angeles County is home to more older adults than most states, spread across a city of nearly 500 square miles and dozens of other cities. That size shapes a bathroom project in practical ways: who issues the permit, how far the installer drives, where the truck parks, and which aging agency you call for help. The common thread is the bathroom itself, and the need to make bathing safer without turning the house upside down.

What Los Angeles homes tend to need

Craftsman and Spanish-style homes from the 1910s to the 1930s

Neighborhoods like Highland Park, West Adams, Mid-City and parts of Pasadena and Glendale are full of early 20th-century houses, many on raised foundations. Bathrooms are often small, with original tile walls and sometimes a heavy cast-iron tub. When that tub comes out, the installer may find old galvanized supply lines or a corroded drain, and the tile walls usually need a new surround. The upside is crawlspace access, which makes plumbing changes easier than in a slab house.

Post-war ranch homes in the Valley and South Bay

The San Fernando Valley, Lakewood, Torrance and many other areas grew quickly after World War II with single-story homes. Many have one hall bathroom with a standard 60-inch tub, which is often a good candidate for a direct swap. Some sit on slabs, which matters only if the drain needs to move.

Hillside homes

Homes in the Hollywood Hills, Mount Washington, Silver Lake or the Palos Verdes Peninsula may have long exterior stairs or steep driveways. Getting a large tub to the bathroom can be the hardest part of the job. Measure the path carefully and send photos of stairs and turns.

Condos and apartments

Many older Angelenos live in condos in Westwood, Wilshire, Marina del Rey or Long Beach, or in apartments. Expect association approval, insurance certificates from the installer, elevator reservations and limited work hours. Renters need the owner's written permission. Our older California homes guide covers HOA and condo steps.

Climate notes

Most of the Los Angeles basin has mild weather, so a cold bathroom is less of an issue than in many places. That changes in the Antelope Valley and the Santa Clarita Valley, where winter nights can be cold and summers hot. In Lancaster or Palmdale, a heated seat may matter more, and so does making sure the water heater can keep up during winter months when incoming water is colder.

Permits in Los Angeles County

There is no single permit office for "Los Angeles." Inside the City of Los Angeles, the Department of Building and Safety (LADBS) issues building permits. Pasadena, Glendale, Burbank, Santa Monica, Long Beach and the other incorporated cities each run their own building departments. Unincorporated areas, such as Altadena, East Los Angeles or Hacienda Heights, are handled by Los Angeles County. A like-for-like swap may be handled differently from a job that adds an electrical circuit, moves a drain or replaces a water heater. Ask your installer which office has jurisdiction and who will pull the permit.

If you replace a water heater as part of the project, California law requires it to be strapped or braced for earthquakes. In a region shaped by the 1971 San Fernando and 1994 Northridge earthquakes, that is one rule nobody minds.

Local aging resources

Los Angeles is unusual in having two Area Agencies on Aging:

  • City of Los Angeles Department of Aging, the AAA for planning and service area 25, serving residents of the city.
  • Los Angeles County Aging & Disabilities Department, the AAA for planning and service area 19, serving the rest of the county.

The two agencies coordinate on a joint plan for the county. Ask them about home safety and fall prevention programs, caregiver support, senior centers with balance classes, and free Medicare counseling. If you are not sure which agency serves an address, call the California Department of Aging's line at 1-800-510-2020.

Funding notes for LA families

  • Veterans can ask the VA Greater Los Angeles Healthcare System's prosthetics office about the HISA benefit before buying anything. VA lists walk-in and roll-in showers as eligible examples and excludes spa or Jacuzzi-type tubs.
  • Medi-Cal members can ask their managed care plan whether it offers home modifications as a Community Support.

A Los Angeles checklist

  • Confirm which city or county office issues your permit.
  • Measure the path, especially stairs on hillside lots.
  • Plan parking for the installation vehicle; street parking rules vary by neighborhood.
  • Get association approval first if you live in a condo.
  • Check the installer's CSLB license.
  • Call your AAA about any fall prevention help available.

Questions people ask

Do you have a showroom in Los Angeles?

No. Consultations start by phone, using your measurements and photos. Professional installation is arranged where available through Aging Safely Baths & More's factory-certified installers, and delivery is available if you have your own licensed contractor.

Who issues permits for a bathroom project in Los Angeles?

Inside the City of Los Angeles, the Department of Building and Safety (LADBS) handles building permits. Other cities in the county, such as Pasadena, Glendale or Long Beach, have their own building departments, and unincorporated areas are handled by the county. Your installer should confirm what applies to your address.

I live in a rent-stabilized apartment. Can I get a walk-in tub?

Only with the owner's written permission, and any changes and who pays should be in writing. Tenants with disabilities may have rights to request reasonable modifications; a local fair housing organization can explain them.

Which aging agency serves my Los Angeles address?

The City of Los Angeles Department of Aging serves the city, and the Los Angeles County Aging & Disabilities Department serves the rest of the county. If you are not sure, call the statewide line at 1-800-510-2020 and it will route you.
