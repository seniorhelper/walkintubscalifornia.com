Inland Empire

Walk-in tubs in Riverside, San Bernardino and the Inland Empire

Many Inland Empire families moved into newer two-story homes where the main bathroom is upstairs. As stairs get harder, the question becomes not only which tub, but which bathroom. Here is how to plan for that, plus local resources in both counties.

The Inland Empire covers two of the largest counties in the country by area, and it has grown quickly for decades. Many households here have lived in the same home for twenty years and are now planning for the next twenty. The bathroom is usually the first room that needs to change.

The upstairs bathroom question

Many homes built from the late 1980s onward in Temecula, Murrieta, Corona, Fontana, Rancho Cucamonga and Moreno Valley have the bedrooms and the primary bath upstairs, with a half bath or a small full bath downstairs. When a parent starts to struggle with stairs, families face a choice:

OptionWorks well whenThings to check
Walk-in tub in the upstairs primary bathThe person still manages stairs safelyFloor framing, path up the stairs for delivery, how stairs will be handled later
Walk-in tub or shower in a downstairs full bathA downstairs full bath existsRoom size, whether a bedroom can move downstairs too
Convert a downstairs half bathThere is space to enlarge itBigger project with plumbing and possibly walls

If stairs are already a problem, think about the whole daily routine. A first-floor bedroom near a bathroom with a walk-in shower may do more for safety than an upstairs tub. Our tub versus shower guide can help you decide.

55+ communities and older neighborhoods

Sun City in Menifee was developed in the 1960s as a retirement community, and Hemet and San Jacinto have many older adults in single-story homes and mobilehome parks. These single-story homes are easier to adapt than two-story tracts, though some have small hall bathrooms. In mobilehome parks, floor strength and permitting are the main things to check; many park alterations are permitted by the state Department of Housing and Community Development. Older neighborhoods in Riverside, Redlands and Upland include early 20th-century homes on raised foundations, which makes plumbing access easier.

Heat and the bathroom

Inland summers are hot, and the High Desert has both hot summers and cold winter nights. A few practical notes:

  • Warm water and a warm house can lower blood pressure. Keep baths moderate, sit for a moment after draining, and stand up slowly.
  • Drink water before a bath during heat waves, as the doctor advises.
  • In the High Desert, a heated seat can make winter drain time more comfortable, and incoming water is colder in winter, which tests the water heater.
  • Hard water is common in many parts of the region. Check your water district's annual report and follow jet cleaning instructions.

Local aging resources

Riverside County

The Riverside County Office on Aging serves the whole county, with information and assistance, caregiver support, care management and senior programs. Visit rcaging.org or call 1-800-510-2020.

San Bernardino County

The Department of Aging and Adult Services provides senior information and assistance, In-Home Supportive Services administration, adult protective services and other programs across the county, from Chino to the High Desert.

Ask either agency about fall prevention programs, evidence-based exercise classes, family caregiver support, and free Medicare counseling.

Veterans

Inland Empire veterans are served by the VA Loma Linda Healthcare System. Contact its prosthetics service about the HISA benefit before you purchase anything. Walk-in and roll-in showers are listed by VA as eligible examples; spa, hot tub and Jacuzzi-type tubs are excluded. See our funding guide.

Permits

Each city in the Inland Empire runs its own building department, and unincorporated areas go through Riverside County or San Bernardino County. Newer cities such as Menifee, Eastvale and Jurupa Valley have their own departments as well. Requirements depend on the scope, so let the installer confirm what applies.

An Inland Empire checklist

  • Decide which bathroom makes sense for the next several years, not just today.
  • If upstairs, have the floor framing checked.
  • Measure the stairway if the tub has to go up it.
  • Check HOA rules in planned communities.
  • Call your county aging agency about fall prevention help.

Questions people ask

My primary bathroom is upstairs. Should the walk-in tub go there?

Not necessarily. If stairs are becoming difficult, a downstairs bathroom may be the better place for a walk-in tub or a walk-in shower, so the person can bathe without climbing. If the only full bath is upstairs, the installer should check the floor framing for the weight of a filled tub.

Which county agency do I call in the Inland Empire?

Riverside County residents can contact the Riverside County Office on Aging, and San Bernardino County residents can contact the Department of Aging and Adult Services. The statewide line, 1-800-510-2020, routes callers to the right agency.

Does the heat matter for a walk-in tub?

It can. In hot summer months, a long warm soak in a warm house can leave some people light-headed. Keep the bathroom cool, use a moderate water temperature, and stand up slowly after draining.

Do you serve the High Desert?

Delivery is available in Victorville, Hesperia, Apple Valley and other High Desert communities. Call with your ZIP code to check professional installation availability.
